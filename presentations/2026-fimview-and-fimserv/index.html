The goal

The goal One place where the group makes, checks and shares flood maps NOAA's HAND method can map a flood for any river reach in the country in minutes. The hard part is everything around it: estimating the discharge, running thousands of watersheds without losing track, knowing what is actually finished, and handing the result to the people who need it. FIMview is the control room for that. Make · Check · Share Return-period flood maps (2 to 500 · See every map on one map, watch · Export GeoPackage, GeoTIFF, years), historical snapshots and · every job, read every log, and score · Shapefile and images for GIS and forecasts, for one watershed, a river · a map against observed floods and · partners, with manifests, or the raw system, a basin or the whole USA. · FEMA benchmarks. · terrain model for study. The method

The method HAND: every cell knows its height above the nearest stream Terrain, computed once Vertical arrows = HAND of that cell (height above the nearest · A 10 m elevation model is drained to the NWM drainage cell) · stream network. Each cell's elevation minus the elevation of the stream cell it drains to is its HAND value. Flooding is a threshold Raise the water to a stage; every cell whose Water surface at a · HAND is below it is wet. No hydrodynamics given stage · are solved, which is why one watershed maps in minutes. Nearest drainage: HAND =0 Flooded where HAND < stage; · Per reach, per catchment depth = stage − HAND · Each NWM reach has its own catchment of cells and its own stage, so a flood map is the union of thousands of small, independent decisions. Map · Lakes

Map · Lakes Lakes: the water the flood map cannot see HAND stops at the shoreline. The engine maps river water over the floodplain and knows nothing about a lake bed, so every reservoir is a blank in the flood map. The Lakes tab fills it: from a DEM that holds the bed and the shore in one grid, it gives the shoreline, surface area, volume and a depth map at any water level. Any reference surface. Levels are quoted in feet or metres on NAVD88, NGVD29 or local mean sea level, with the shift looked up at the lake from NGS and NOAA, or on a custom datum; live levels come from the USGS gauge or Reclamation. Where this goes: merge the two depth surfaces, river flooding from the FIM and lake depth at the lake's level, into one continuous water map of a flood. Shown: Lake Houston, the Harvey study area. Bed from TWDB's 2018 survey, terrain from USGS 3DEP. Appendix · The equations, in plain words

Appendix · The equations, in plain words Five formulas carry the whole pipeline Step · Formula · In words Return-period flow (LP-III) · log QT = mean + KT × spread · Take the logarithm of each year's biggest flow. The T-year flow is the average of QT = 10log QT · those plus a frequency factor KT (set by T and the skew) times their spread. Undo the logarithm. Rating curve (Manning) · Q = (1 / n) × A × R2/3 × √S · For each possible stage, the channel's wet area A, its hydraulic radius R, its slope S and a roughness n give a discharge. The hydrotable is this computed for 84 stages per reach; the engine reads it backwards: flow in, stage out. Flood test (the engine) · depth = stage − HAND · A cell floods when the water in its reach stands higher than the cell sits above that wet if depth > 0.03 m · reach. That subtraction, once per cell, is the entire flood map. Rain to runoff (curve number) · S = 1000 / CN − 10 · A curve number says how much a landscape soaks up (S); a fifth of that is lost before runoff = (P − 0.2 S)² / (P + 0.8 S) · anything runs off. What is left of the rain P becomes runoff depth, more steeply as the ground saturates. Runoff to peak flow (NRCS) · qp = 484 × A × runoff / Tp · Spread the runoff over the basin's area A and the time its water takes to arrive (Tc, Tp ≈ 0.67 × Tc · from flow length and slope); the peak is the runoff volume divided by about two thirds of that time. Scoring a map (CSI) · CSI = hits / (hits + false alarms + misses) · Of everything that either the map or the observation called flooded, the share both agreed on. 1 is perfect; a map that floods everything or nothing scores near 0. Units in the deck: flows in m³/s, stages and HAND in metres (the rasters store millimetres), rainfall in inches where the NRCS method is quoted, areas in km² for maps and mi² inside the NRCS formula.
